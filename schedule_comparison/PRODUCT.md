# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Construction and civil engineering project controls engineers, schedule analysts, and project managers who live in Primavera P6. They are technical, deadline-driven, and deeply fluent in schedule terminology (float, critical path, WBS, data date, earned value). They use this tool under pressure — reviewing updates, preparing reports for clients, or diagnosing schedule risk. They trust data over decoration.

## Product Purpose

A web-based schedule comparison dashboard that accepts two Primavera P6 XER files (baseline + updated) and instantly produces a structured variance report across 10 analytical tabs: Overview, Schedule Variance, KPIs, Lookahead, Milestones, Procurement, Earned Value, WBS, Logic, and AI Chat. It replaces hours of manual P6 comparison work with a shareable, permanent URL.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, Modern, Fast. Confident like Linear, precise like Stripe, dense with useful information. No chrome for chrome's sake.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards: cookie-cutter indigo/white card grids, identical card rows, hero metric templates, gradient text, glassmorphism panels
- AI slop tells: big colorful number + small label + gradient accent, endless identical stat cards, shadow-heavy glassmorphism
- Tableau/Power BI: overcrowded chart dashboards, enterprise-gray aesthetic, decorative data viz for its own sake

## Design Principles

1. **Data over decoration** — every pixel earns its place by surfacing schedule intelligence. Reduce chrome, not content.
2. **Status at a glance** — critical, late, at-risk information must be legible in 2 seconds, not after reading.
3. **Expert confidence** — the UI assumes the user knows P6. No tooltips explaining what "float" means. Trust the professional.
4. **Precision without pedantry** — numbers are exact, labels are terse, information hierarchy is strict. Avoid restated headings and intros.
5. **Responsive under pressure** — the tool loads fast, renders immediately, and never makes the user wait or wonder.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Focus indicators required for keyboard navigation across tabs, tables, and chat. Color must never be the sole carrier of status information — badge text and icons are required alongside color. Reduced motion respected for transitions.
